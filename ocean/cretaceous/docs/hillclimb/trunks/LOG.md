# trunks: hill-climb log

One line per pass: the fault, the change, kept or reverted, why. Views: the
gallery's LOD-match capture of 11 trees (`CRET_GALLERY_MATCH`, at 3 m and
30 m, from the shaded and the sunlit side; `CRET_GALLERY_MATCH_DIST`,
`_AZ` and `_FRAMES` were added for this), the tree gallery pages, and the
island world shots.

1. Every species shared four 512 px bark scans, drawn at about half their real size (a tile 0.8 to 1 m round a trunk). Added four bark layers (araucaria plates `pine_bark`, podocarp strips `japanese_cedar_bark`, ginkgo furrows `bark_brown_02`, plane flakes `bark_platanus`; the general broadleaf now `chinese_hackberry_bark`), fetched at 2K, and each tile laid at the scan's own size (1 to 1.8 m). Kept: neutral in shade, but the plates and strips read at their real size once lit (pass 3).
2. Trunks were smooth cones: a faceted skirt at the foot (redwood) or nothing, radial normals. Added a bark relief by species on every trunk (root flare with 4 to 8 buttress roots, flutes fading up the trunk, bumps and burls), trunk rings every 0.25 m in the bottom 2 m, more sides for the flutes, normals from the surface itself. Kept: the redwood foot is a real flared, rooted base, and every trunk now widens into the ground instead of standing on it like a post.
3. The bark was near black in sun and shade (median sRGB 18 on the redwood) and its texture invisible: dark targets (0.05 to 0.11 linear), a grey-brown tone multiplied on top, and the shader's cavity term taking another 20 to 33%. Each scan now has its own furrow shade baked in at fetch time (its AO times the low end of its height map), the shader's cavity term is a third as strong on bark, the conifer and broadleaf tones are 1, and each species' mean bark albedo is set to a real one (redwood 0.17/0.088/0.056, plane 0.19/0.18/0.145). Kept: plates on the araucaria, red fibrous strips on the redwood, grey furrows on the ginkgo, from both sides. (A probe showed the lit side is not self-shadowed: a high sun just grazes a trunk.)
4. No stubs or scars: every trunk was clean from the flare to the crown. Added broken-off branch stubs (1 to 5 by species, 1.6 m to a third of the way up, most of them weathered grey), the same on both meshes. First try (3 to 7 cm, up to the middle of the trunk) was invisible at 30 m and hidden in the crowns; made thicker and lower. Kept: a few nubs on the bare trunk at 30 m, like the pruned-off limbs on the reference trunks.
5. The plane's bark was a uniform grey-brown, nothing like a plane. Its trunk and limbs now carry a camouflage of cream, grey-green and olive-brown patches in the shader (in metres round the plant, sharp up close, soft once small). Kept: at 30 m the trunk reads as a plane's pale mottled bark; the first, gentler version was too faint to see.
6. Cycad and tree-fern trunks were black smooth cones. The cycad's diamond leaf bases are now raised, domed scales in the shading (a bump from the same pattern), and the tree fern's trunk a mat of wavering root fibres; the cycad bark is lighter and the tree-fern bark a little lighter. Kept: under a low sun the armour and the fibres catch the light; under a high sun the crowns shade these trunks and it is barely visible (no worse).
7. The stubs stood out level like pegs and the plane's cream was cold and covered most of the trunk. Stubs now rise 10 to 45 degrees from a collar to a splintered point; the plane's cream is warmer and covers less. Kept: the stubs read as broken branches at 3 m.
8. Tree-fern trunks were smooth cones (Dicksonia a squat one). They now take the bark relief (a swelling root mass at the foot, a shaggy, lumpy mantle up the trunk, rings every 0.25 m, twice the sides on the full mesh), and the Dicksonia trunk is a column that narrows only a little. Kept: under a low sun both read as fibrous columns.
9. Limbs thinned too fast and leapt from the trunk with no join (at 10 m, thin dark sticks). Limbs drawn through `bark()` are 20% heavier than grown (the skeleton keeps its points) and swell into a collar where they leave their parent. Kept: at 10 m the ginkgo's and the plane's limbs carry weight and meet their trunk in a collar. Also: snags without flutes, and the reduced mesh splits only the flare, to stay in the triangle budget.
