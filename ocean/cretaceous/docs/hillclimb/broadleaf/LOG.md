# Broadleaf hill-climb log

One line per pass: the worst fault seen, the change, kept or reverted, and why.

1. Fault: broadleaf trees were 5 to 14 m (magnolia 8-13, laurel 7-11, katsura 9-14, plane 12-17, ginkgo 10-15), bushes next to the conifers. Change: real heights (ginkgo 16-22 m, katsura 13-18, laurel 9-13, plane 21-27, magnolia 11-16, chloranth 3.5-5.5; trunk girth about 1/25-1/40 of the height), and `crown_scale` grows each crown's twig spacing, cluster size and card count with the tree so a tall crown is not sparser, the reduced mesh keeping as many (longer) cards. Kept: the trees now stand at tree height in the gallery; crowns still read sparse (next pass).
2. Fault: crowns were sparse sprigs strung along the skeleton, lopsided on the plane. Change: `Crown.clump`, a domed mass of leaf points round the outer part of each branch (open below), so foliage gathers in clouds with shade between. Kept: laurel and magnolia read as round full crowns, the plane's crown fills both sides.
3. Fault: the full mesh's crown was see-through next to its reduced mesh, and two thirds of its triangles were 3-sided twig tubes hidden inside the crown. Change: `crown_full` draws every other twig and spends the triangles on 1.5 times the leaf cards (the reduced mesh keeps its card count). Kept: denser full crowns at the same cost.
4. Fault: cycads were knee to head high and tree ferns 3-7 m (the stout one 2-4 m): bushes on a stub. Change: Cycas trunks 0.9-2.2 m and 2.2-3.8 m (the tall form), squat cycads and Williamsonia a little taller, Cyathea trunks 4.5-8 m and Dicksonia 2.2-4.2 m, and tree-fern fronds a little longer on the taller trunks. Kept: they stand as small trees in the gallery; the tree-fern crowns still read as a dark dome (next).
5. Fault: tree-fern fronds bent back on themselves (tip angle e0 - 1.5 x droop reached -2.6 rad), so every crown was a tight dark mop. Change: droop 0.8-1.05 (was 1.4-2.1) and the oldest fronds leave lower: fronds rise, run out to level and drop their tips. Kept: an open spreading rosette, the stout fern a shallow vase.
6. Fault: tree-fern fronds read as fat paddles. Change: fronds 15% longer with pinnae at 0.32 of the frond (was 0.4). Reverted: barely visible at 15 m, not clearly better.
7. Fault: the reduced mesh drew the taller crowns with 2-3 m merged cards (giant leaves at 30-45 m) and spent most of its triangles on branches hidden in the crown (over its 1.25x budget). Change: merged cards k^0.6 longer and k^0.8 as many (k = height over the tuned height), and the reduced mesh drops branches under 2.5 cm x height/10 instead of 2.5 cm. Kept: finer, fuller reduced crowns at true leaf scale, every reduced broadleaf within 1.25x of its old count.
8. Fault: the ginkgo was a pole with short level limbs and a few leaves near their tips (photos: long limbs leaving the trunk low and rising steeply, leaf tufts all along them). Change: fork at 14-22% of the height, limbs at 57 degrees turning up, a wider envelope, fewer heavier limbs, `Crown.inner` 0.7 so spur-shoot leaves sit along the limbs and not only on the surface; every third twig drawn to stay in budget. Kept: reads as an upright vase of ascending limbs; still the most open crown, as the real tree is.
9. Fault: the laurel was a loose round head on a bare stick at mid height (photos of Laurus: a dense, dark, upright oval crown from low down). Change: fork at 70% of the old height, limbs at 57 degrees, a narrower envelope reaching lower (centre 0.56 H, half height 0.46 H), 1250 cards with leaves inside the crown too (`inner` 0.35). A first try (fork at 55%, limbs at 49 degrees) made a shrub; kept the second: an upright dense oval on a short trunk.
10. Fault: on the new 2-3.8 m trunks the Cycas crown was the knee-high plant's tuft, a thin feather duster on a pole. Change: fronds 12% longer and 3 more per metre of trunk over 1.2 m. Kept: the tall cycad carries a full rosette of stiff fronds.
11. Fault: broadleaf crowns look evenly lit, without dark hollows between their masses. Change: crown extinction for the sky-light bake 0.8 (was 0.5) on broadleaves. Reverted: in sun the direct light and shadow maps dominate; the gallery pages changed by a few values, no visible gain.
12. Fault: the katsura was a narrow dense oval, a twin of the laurel beside it (Cercidiphyllum: a broad vase of several stems). Change: crown envelope 0.98 of the broadleaf width (was 0.72), centred a little lower, 1350 cards (1000) on the full mesh, the reduced mesh's count unchanged. A first try without the extra cards was broad but thin. Kept: a broad, full, many-stemmed vase, clearly not the laurel.
13. Fault: the river plane is a loose, see-through umbrella of thin branches (photos: a tall dense dome of dark clumped masses). Change: rounder, taller envelope, 920 cards with clump 0.9, every fourth twig drawn to pay for them, fewer longer reduced cards. Reverted: the full crown was taller but no denser, and the reduced crown went back to giant leaves.
14. Fault: the river plane's crown is still the most see-through. Change: the same envelope with 950 cards (700) and clump 0.9, every fourth twig, the reduced mesh's cards unchanged. Reverted: at 30 m the crown looked the same; its openness comes from its leaf-cluster sprite (atlas, not this track), not the card count.
15. Fault: the magnolia was the same spreading round head as every other broadleaf (photos of Magnolia grandiflora: a central leader under a tall, dense oval crown carried low). Change: excurrent (leader) with limbs from half the old fork height, a narrower, taller envelope, 1000 cards with some inside the crown; the reduced mesh keeps its card count. Kept: a dense upright oval, now told apart from the laurel and the plane at a glance.
16. Fault: tree-fern crowns were thin plates of about 20 fronds (photos of Dicksonia and Cyathea: 30-50 fronds in a full shuttlecock). Change: the full mesh adds half as many fronds again between the others, on a forked stream so the reduced mesh (its fewer fronds already carry wider pinnae) stays the same plant; the stout fern's fronds rise more steeply (a vase). Kept: full rosettes at 9 m; full, reduced and impostor still match at 30 m. The full tree-fern mesh is now 12-14k triangles (was 7.4-8.8k); all meshes with the crease are +7.3% vertices and +11.4% triangles, under test_veg's 8% and 12%.
17. Fault: tree-fern pinnae read as broad serrated leaves. Change: pinna cards 0.28 as wide as long (0.38) on the full mesh. Reverted: a little lacier at 9 m, not clearly; the paddle look is the pinna sprite's own outline.
18. Fault: the chloranth (brief: a 3-6 m shrub) was a lollipop: a round head on one bare stem. Change: 2-3 stems from the ground, crown from a fifth of the height, a wider, lower envelope, 520 cards with some inside; fewer limbs and one more card skipped on the reduced mesh to stay in budget. Kept: a many-stemmed shrub, leafy from knee height, as Hedyosmum grows.
19. Fault: the tree fern's dead skirt was a thin sheaf of 5-8 fronds hanging straight down the trunk. Change: 8-12 dead fronds (5 on the reduced mesh), leaving the trunk at 40-66 degrees down and drooping, slightly longer and wider (longer still on the stout fern). Kept: a flared skirt under the crown, as on Dicksonia; it still reads dark olive in the crown's shade rather than brown (the dry tint is the plant shader's, not this track's).
20. Fault: broadleaf crowns shade evenly top to side. Change: leaf cards lean more toward the sky (upbias 0.45, was 0.25) on magnolia, laurel, katsura and chloranth. Reverted: no visible difference at 25 m; the bent normals of the sky bake already decide the shading.
