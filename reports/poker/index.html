01 / FOUNDATION

Game & betting

Implemented

No-limit Texas Hold’em with independent 200 BB hands, 50/100 blinds, rotating button, no rake or ante. Stacks reset after every hand.

  • One C engine, compiled for 2, 6, or 9 seats. The default trainer is six-seat; Slumbot evaluation is heads-up.
  • Tracks stacks, street bets, total contributions, turn order, and who can still act. Supports unequal starting stacks and side pots.
  • A raise is a total commitment on this street. Calling pays only the difference; a short stack calls all-in.
Betting rules & correctness evidence

The minimum full raise-to is current high bet + last full raise increment. A full raise updates that increment; a short all-in preserves it. Cumulative short raises can reopen action once a player’s full threshold is met.

Example: with the small blind at 50, a raise-to of 250 puts in 200 more chips, for a total of 2.5 BB on this street.

  • Folding is unavailable when checking is free. Heads-up: button posts the small blind, acts first preflop, and last postflop.
  • A lone player with chips acts only if facing a call; otherwise the board runs out. A short posted big blind retains the nominal 100-chip opening price when multiple players can still bet.
  • Settlement handles uncalled returns, folded contributions, all-in side pots, and split pots. Odd chips go one at a time clockwise from the button.
  • Exact 5–7 card ranking uses bit masks. The hot path has no allocations or large hand-rank lookup tables.

Recorded checks include 16 Bend model laws, sanitizer runs of 20,032 hands per seat count, 20,000 independent rank samples, and 1,000 PokerKit shared-action hands per seat count. Short-raise and odd-chip convention differences are documented. These are model proofs plus implementation tests, not a proof of the entire C engine.

02 / REPRESENTATION

What the neural policy sees

Implemented

A handwritten float vector, relative to the observing seat. It contains own hole cards, the visible board, public chip state, and recent public actions. A learned dense layer projects it into the network.

observation width = 16 + 15 × seats
HU: 46  ·  6 seats: 106  ·  9 seats: 151 floats
  • 16 base fields: street, pot, high bet, raise increment, relative button, live count, own cards, visible board, turn/count/terminal flags.
  • 7 per seat: stack, street bet, total committed, starting stack, and live/active/pending flags.
  • 2 × seats event rows: four fields per event—relative actor, street, action kind, and street commitment.
Encoding, history & privacy

Cards are scalar IDs 4 × rank + suit, with ranks 23456789TJQKA and suits cdhs, divided by 52. Unrevealed board cards use the value 1. Chips are divided by a fixed 20,000, even for other starting stack sizes.

Street and event kind are divided by 3; live count and event actors by seat count; button by seats − 1; decision count by 1,024. Real event actors use (relative seat + 1) / seats; unused rows use zero.

The event window contains actions since this seat’s previous decision, including its own previous action. Recurrent memory stays keyed by table and seat and resets at a new hand. Opponent hole cards and future board cards do not enter observations.

The legal-action mask is supplied separately. This float encoder serves neural decisions; the handwritten baseline uses the C state directly.

03 / ACTION INTERFACE

Exact bets, eight policy choices

Implemented

The engine accepts exact legal integer bets. The neural policy chooses one of eight slots; a per-decision lookup turns that index into the actual chip amount.

SlotMeaningKernel value
0Fold−1
1Check / call0
2Minimum raiseHigh bet + full increment
3–6⅓, ⅔, 1, 2 × potHigh bet + fraction of pot after calling
7All-inOwn street bet + remaining stack
Legality, clipping & an important distinction

Raise candidates are clamped to the minimum raise and then to the player’s maximum commitment. Duplicate amounts are masked out. Fold is masked out when no call is due; raises are masked out when action is not reopened or no other player can contest a raise.

For a pot fraction f, the candidate is high + f × (pot + amount to call), with integer rounding. The final positive value is a street total, not an incremental payment.

Slumbot can send arbitrary legal bet sizes, and the engine can replay them. The rules bot also returns exact amounts directly: its 2.5 BB opening size need not be present among the neural slots.

04 / CURRENT BOT

A fixed rules baseline

Evaluated

Style 1 is a conservative, randomized heuristic bot. It scores its cards and call price, then folds, calls, or chooses a bet size. It has no search or trained weights.

  • Preflop: rank, pair, suitedness, connectivity, and pressure from the current bet.
  • Postflop: made-hand category, pair quality, draw bonuses, number of live players, and call price.
  • Sizing: open to 250 chips; preflop re-raise to 3 × the high bet; postflop raise by ⅔ of the pot after calling. Clamp to legal bounds.
Frozen identity & scope

Style 0 is the initial variant; style 2 adds a positional variant. Style 1 was retained as a starting baseline after small pilots, not established as the strongest style. Its strength scores are heuristics, not calibrated equity estimates.

The 4,000-hand cumulative measurement pins the same heads-up binary at build/poker/rules_v0.so, with style 1. The filename predates the selected style; it does not mean style 0.

05 / NEURAL PATH

Encoder → MinGRU → policy/value

Smoke-tested

The existing Puffer PPO learner is wired to the CPU environment. Default PufferNet configuration: 64 hidden units, 2 MinGRU layers, eight action logits, and a value output.

  • Float observation → learned dense projection → recurrent layers → action/value head. The legal mask controls which actions can be sampled.
  • The learner uses the GPU; simulation and current frozen-opponent inference run on the CPU.
  • Reward is terminal net chips / 100, in BB; zero intermediate reward, gamma = 1, no reward shaping.
PPO adapter & snapshot league

One fixed learner seat per table; the button rotates. Opponents act until the learner’s next turn. A terminal payoff is recorded even when a hand ends before the learner can make a decision, without inventing an RL transition.

league.py trains in stints. The first field uses rules opponents; later stints add frozen learner snapshots. Opponent identity is sampled once per seat per hand; weights stay frozen during a stint. Default mix is 25% rules, with up to eight snapshots. Learner weights carry forward, but the optimizer restarts between stints.

Recorded validation includes two 4,096-step smoke stints: first against rules, then against a field containing a saved network. This verifies the pipeline, not playing strength. No substantial Puffer training run or Puffer-trained policy Slumbot result exists yet. The external Fullhouse learned policy is evaluated separately below.

The dense acting-seat request/response API exists for a future collector. GPU batching of frozen opponents, dedicated exploiters, and payoff-matrix scheduling are not implemented.

06 / EXTERNAL EVALUATION

How we play Slumbot

Implemented

Python handles HTTPS; the native heads-up library chooses actions. The client replays Slumbot’s public action history into our engine, asks the rules bot for a move, and sends it back.

  • Latest run: one process, 50 concurrent games. Each game has its own HTTP session, token, state, and policy RNG. Requests within a game are sequential.
  • No fixed request delay. Rate limits honor Retry-After; transient failures back off.
  • Append-only JSONL stores completed results, actions, RNG state, binary hash, errors, and summaries. Progress and ETA update every 25 hands.
  • Added October 17: schema 2 records cards and decision traces for future runs. The historical 4,000-hand log contains no cards.
Failure handling, resume & contact

An ambiguous failed action is never replayed: record its unknown outcome, back off, and start a fresh game session. Six consecutive failed hand attempts stop the run; non-transient failures also stop it. A later action cannot be used to silently infer a missing payoff.

--resume keeps completed results and continues to the requested cumulative hand count. It verifies the style and library hash. SIGINT finishes active hands before stopping. An even allocation of hand pairs helps balance positions, though interruptions can disturb that balance.

Requests identify us as:
PufferTank-PokerEval/1.0 (+https://github.com/FelixAllistar)

The runner uses new_hand and act at https://slumbot.com/slumbot/api/. Dependencies are Python, requests, and the native HU shared library. Networking stays outside training.

10 / INVESTIGATION 01 · 2026-10-17

The rules bot folds far too readily

Reproduced offline

The existing sample and frozen-bot probes point to a badly calibrated postflop defense rule. The client audit found no turn-order or bet-amount mismatch in the completed histories. These results concern the handwritten baseline; neural training has not produced this behavior.

What happened in the 4,000 recorded hands

When facing a bet or raiseFoldedRate
Flop496 / 585 decisions84.8%
Turn378 / 481 decisions78.6%
River180 / 280 decisions64.3%

Hands ending in a fold netted −2,180.54 BB across both players’ folds. Showdowns netted −507.23 BB. About 81% of the observed net loss therefore came from pots ending without a showdown.

We won 217 showdowns and lost 185, with 24 ties, but losing showdowns were larger on average: 19.61 BB lost versus 14.38 BB won. The big blind lost 88.92 BB/100; the small blind lost 45.14 BB/100.

What the exact frozen binary does

Synthetic legal spotStyle-1 action
A♥ K♥ on Q♥ 7♥ 2♣; call 1 BB with 41 BB already in the potFold, 1,000 / 1,000 RNG states
A♠ A♦ on K♥ 7♣ 2♦; facing a pot-sized flop betFold, 1,000 / 1,000 RNG states
A♠ K♦ in the BB versus a 2.5 BB openCall, 1,000 / 1,000 RNG states; never re-raise

These are reproducible decision probes, not recovered cards from old hands. They show blanket rules that discard strong draws and overpairs, plus very passive preflop re-raising. A particular fold can be correct against a particular range; this bot has no such range model.

Fold losses are accounting, not the amount a fix would recover. We have concrete policy weaknesses, but cannot assign a precise share of the historical loss to each weakness without the missing cards and counterfactual action values.
The faulty thresholds and what the audit rules out

The postflop rule compares an arbitrary strength score with integer pot-odds percent + 25, with an extra penalty for large calls. Those scores have never been calibrated to winning probability.

  • A high-card flush draw receives 14 + 12 − 5 = 21. The fold threshold is at least 25 even for a tiny bet, so a bare flush draw always folds when facing a bet. The probe’s call costs just 2.38% of the pot after calling.
  • An overpair on the dry flop receives 43 + 15 − 5 = 53. A pot-sized bet produces a threshold of 33 + 25 = 58, so even aces fold. The fold test runs before the randomized raise test.
  • AK offsuit scores 39 preflop; the first randomized re-raise gate starts at 43. The sample made only 36 three-bets across 1,937 opportunities to re-raise a first preflop raise (1.86%).

All 16,836 recorded actions replayed legally. An independent comparison with Slumbot’s official ParseAction agreed on actor, active street, and current street bet at every action prefix. All 3,574 fold payoffs matched independently accumulated contributions; all 426 showdown payouts had a valid stake magnitude.

This checks protocol interpretation and chip accounting. It cannot verify the showdown winner without the cards, and it does not prove every engine or client path correct. The original 23 unresolved attempts retain their uncertainty.

Logging repair completed; strategy still frozen

The original logger omitted hole cards and boards in all 4,000 historical completed records. Those cards cannot be recovered from the saved betting strings. This was a gap in our evaluation tooling.

Schema 2 now records own cards, visible board, public history, pot and call amounts, stack, abstract legal amounts, exact chosen action, and RNG before/after each decision. Terminal records retain opponent cards when revealed. Interrupted attempts retain the same available trace. Session tokens remain excluded.

Five offline client tests pass, including an exact two-street decision replay and a check that later board/opponent revelations do not enter an earlier decision’s trace. No new live match or policy change was made for this investigation.

11 / SIX-PLAYER DIRECTION · CHECKED 2026-10-17

Six-player bots exist; keep six-player evaluation

Research notes

Our engine already supports six seats. Slumbot is useful for debugging and an independent heads-up score, but a strong heads-up result alone does not establish six-player strength. Position, opening ranges, multiway hand value, and bluff incentives change.

ProjectWhat is availableWhat we know so far
PluribusPublished six-player research result against professional humans.Establishes that strong six-player AI is possible; not an integrated opponent in our setup.
fullhouse-botSix-max Deep CFR runtime, local benchmark, and 577 KB weight artifact.Now ported and benchmarked below. It beats our original bot locally heads-up, but has not established a six-player upgrade or a winning Slumbot result.
QuantPlaySix-max browser/local trainer with a pool of heuristic opponents.Useful for playing or assembling a varied test field. Coaching grades compare with its own engine, rather than certifying optimal play.
NoRegretsPublic Rust implementation for 2–6 players; training and search tools.Its current README says pretrained blueprints are commercial-only. Public source can train its own; not a ready free pretrained benchmark.
Transfer limits and one misleading repository name

Basic card evaluation, chip accounting, action legality, and the confirmed bad-fold rules matter in both formats. Strategy quality still needs separate tests at six seats. A six-seat hand that gets heads-up retains its original positions and earlier betting information.

Our current neural input is 46 floats for HU versus 106 for six seats. Checkpoints are seat-count-specific, so a trained HU network cannot simply be loaded as a six-seat network.

The repository now named CFR-NLHE-6max explicitly says its current released models are heads-up and the six-player name is historical. It is not currently the six-player pretrained alternative its name suggests.

What changed, parity checks, and native speed

Fullhouse is pinned to commit e504793. The port preserves its 51 features, two 256-unit trunk layers, five-action menu, regret matching, and short-stack push/fold chart. No separately trained HU checkpoint was supplied; the same runtime accepts two or six seats. Model inputs use public state and own cards only. The port uses the native RNG with the same action probabilities.

The final parity suite matched 2,896 feature vectors exactly, probabilities within 0.00000365, 2,000 eval7 hand ranks, 910 short-stack decisions, and 71 hidden-card permutations. Expanded testing caught and corrected the upstream dict encoder’s treatment of opening all-ins below one BB. Replaying the corrected port against all 11,048 recorded Fullhouse decisions changed no action or RNG outcome. All 8,904 fresh original-policy decisions also replay exactly in the new native library.

Embedded fp32 weights and equity tables total 588,180 bytes. No Python, BLAS, heap allocation, GPU, or network is needed for native decisions. Initial warm latency samples ranged roughly 28–66 µs native versus 503–1,092 µs Python on the shared i5-7400 host. A six-player all-Fullhouse match measured 33,012 decisions/s and 2,876 hands/s. The focused rules candidate against five originals measured 11.74 million decisions/s and 1.38 million hands/s. These are different match workloads, and host load varied; use them as measured throughput snapshots.

Public street aggression counters add two bytes per simulator table, making current native state 86 / 190 / 268 bytes for 2 / 6 / 9 seats. Observation dimensions and checkpoint shapes are unchanged. The older speed table retains its historical state sizes.

Style 5 preserves the old thresholds for most hands, while allowing appropriately priced draws and top-pair/overpair defense and adding AK re-raises. The draw allowance and made-hand thresholds remain heuristics, not range equity or a solved strategy. Both confirmed bad-fold probes now continue for all 1,000 tested RNG states.

13 / WORKING DIRECTION · 2026-10-17

Improve playing strength; keep the approach open.

Six-player poker remains the main interest, with Slumbot as a useful heads-up benchmark. We have not committed to hand-written rules, a particular learning method, or an external bot. No formal automated goal has been created. The user has authorized benchmarking native opponents and testing improvements in both formats.

The proposed role for the rules bot is a cheap, reproducible baseline and one opponent in a varied field. Fixing its demonstrated mistakes can be useful without making extensive rule tuning the main project. Investigate compact learned policies and available opponents alongside that work. Training cost and inference model size are separate considerations; the small Fullhouse weight artifact above is a concrete example, but does not establish its playing strength.

The first native benchmark round is complete. Keep the original default and the measured alternatives available; the focused revision needs stronger evidence before promotion. Longer term, realistic human practice needs persistent opponent tendencies and table history. A large policy count alone would not establish human realism: our present rules and Fullhouse baselines have no cross-hand memory, and the learner currently resets recurrent state at each hand.

Next experiments to review

  1. Audit the deployed Fullhouse checkpoint. The supplied deep_cfr_model.npz contains a single advantage network, which our port matches. The pinned training code exports an average-strategy network by default and offers advantage-network export as an option. Check whether an averaged checkpoint is available and whether the supplied artifact matches the intended training/deployment setup before investing in more tuning. This is an open question, not a confirmed defect or explanation of the losses. Pinned export code. The original Deep CFR algorithm learns an average strategy; its equilibrium guarantee concerns two-player zero-sum games and does not establish six-player strength. Deep CFR paper.
  2. Test bounded equity estimation against opponent ranges. Replace the rule bot's arbitrary hand-strength score with sampled showdown equity against plausible hands, conditioned on position and public betting. Compare a small fixed budget, such as 32 versus 128 samples, against the same duplicate-deal fields in both formats, measuring native throughput as well as returns. These budgets are proposals, not measured results. Equity alone is not action value: future betting, fold equity, and multiway pots still need separate treatment. Keep an untouched test seed/field for the chosen candidate before another Slumbot comparison.
  3. Make opponents persist and adapt across hands. For human practice, start with a small varied population whose tendencies persist per seat, plus public-history statistics such as voluntary entry, preflop raises, responses to aggression, and bet sizes. Test that changes in table history produce the intended behavior without hidden-card access. Treat strength, behavioral diversity, and similarity to human play as separate measurements; the last requires an actual human reference dataset. A larger population alone does not answer that question.
