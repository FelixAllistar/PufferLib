Why the current comparison isn't fair yet

Different GPUs

PufferNet was swept on the local GTX 1060 (3 GB visible), the transformer on a 16 GB RTX 5060 Ti. Memory limits shaped which settings PufferNet could even try.

Different starting points

The transformer sweep's first trial was PufferNet's tuned winner; PufferNet's sweep started from the old 32×2 defaults. Search ranges were otherwise identical.

Exact exploiter

Both transformer winners train against exact best-response opponents. PufferNet never really tested that option, so 0.037 vs 0.060 mixes architecture with training setup.

CUDA graphs off

The transformer sweep inherited cudagraphs = 0 from the config, which costs a lot of speed. Graphs were turned off because they used too much GPU memory; that needs a real fix.

Budget

We train 50M steps per run. The paper reaches 0.007 in 2M steps on what is very likely the same game: our rules are OpenSpiel's default 4-card Goofspiel (past bids visible, random prizes, win/loss), and both compute uniform play at exploitability 0.7465. So the gap is in our training setup, not the game.

PPO-EMAg, as the paper defines it

Source: EMAgnet (Maidment et al., arXiv 2606.23995), Section 3.1, Algorithm 1 and Table 2.

L(θ) = L_PPO(θ)
     + λ_KL · E_z[ KL( π_mag(·|z) ‖ π_θ(·|z) ) ]

after each PPO epoch (one full pass
over the rollout's minibatches):
  θ_mag ← (1 − τ) θ_mag + τ θ

init: θ_mag ← θ (same random weights)

Update timing

Once per pass over the buffer, not once per rollout and not after every gradient step. With our replay ratio of ~4–7, that is ~4–7 magnet updates per rollout.

Paper's search ranges

λ_KL 0.01–32, τ 1e-5–0.1, entropy 1e-4–0.1 (anneal on/off), lr 1e-7–0.01, clip 1e-4–0.4, GAE λ 0.6–1, VF coef 0.1–5. 700 samples × 3 seeds per method.

Evaluate both policies

The magnet itself often beats the last iterate (0.007 vs 0.015 in 4-card Goofspiel). We should save and score the magnet every eval.

Our EMAg design

Reuse the existing teacher-KL code (ocean/kaggriculture_direct/teacher.cuh). It already computes KL(teacher ‖ learner) per minibatch with its own recurrent state; it needs to become generic and gain the EMA update.

Decisions to make, in order

1. Pause or go

Either pause Goofspiel now (stop the Vast sweep, keep its results) or commit to the full plan below. The steps after this one assume go.

2. Match the paper's game (done)

Our game is OpenSpiel's default 4-card Goofspiel and our exploitability is NashConv/2, the same as OpenSpiel's. Uniform play scores 0.7465 in both; the other variants score 0.708, 0.750 or 1.25. The paper doesn't print its variant, but its curves start near 1, consistent with a random network on this game. Treat 0.007 as a real target.

3. Implement and test EMAg (done)

Commit 9ccfbff33 on branch magnet-kl adds train.magnet_coef, magnet_tau, anneal_magnet_coef, min_magnet_coef_ratio and base.magnet_path, and saves <ckpt>.magnet. With the magnet off, training is bit-identical. τ=0 holds the anchor; τ=1 tracks the learner. Commit 1bcf54962 removes the old Kaggriculture teacher. Still to do: score the magnet's exploitability in Goofspiel evals.

4. Transformer speed (measured)

On the 5060 Ti, CUDA graphs on and off give identical speed and peak memory for both winning shapes. The transformer is simply about 12× more compute per step than PufferNet (61.6K vs 726K steps/s). Faster attention kernels are a separate project.

5. Mega sweep (running)

PufferNet ×1000, then Transformer ×500, on the same box and with the same ranges. 39 dimensions, including run length (2M–105M steps) and the four magnet settings at the paper's ranges. Ranges are widened where earlier winners hit an edge. The sweep metric is the best of the learner and its magnet. Config and notes: reports/goofspiel_plan/mega_sweep_20261020/.
